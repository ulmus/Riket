# Realms

This is a working draft of a simple fantasy game based on [[Grundregler|I Rikets Tjänst]]. It uses the same d12 dice-pool engine, with characters built around **Profession**, **Ancestry** and a small number of **Abilities**.

## Tone and setting

The world is entering an early Renaissance. Old noble families and feudal oaths remain, but cities, merchant houses and guilds have become powers of their own. Printed works spread dangerous ideas. Surgeons study the body. Mercenaries march with pikes, crossbows and black-powder weapons.

It is a grounded and dangerous world:

- Characters are capable but mortal.
- A drawn weapon means someone may die.
- Wounds take time to heal, and magic does not easily erase their consequences.
- The supernatural remains rare enough to be frightening.
- Most people try to lead ordinary lives. Not every Vicar is a cultist, and not every forest conceals a monster.

Ancient and alien gods move behind the world. They are not humans with larger bodies, but wills whose intentions are difficult to understand. Pagans worship them under old names. Magicians bargain with them for power. Their cults offer cures, knowledge and protection. The price often becomes clear much later.

Characters may be mercenaries, envoys, indebted specialists, hunters or scholars sent where a prince's soldiers are insufficient. They face border disputes, missing merchants and restless graves. Something older waits behind the comprehensible.

See [[World and History]] for the Ascendancy, the Withering and the powers surrounding the Realm. See [[The Others]] for the Four Courts and their Lesser Others.

## Core principles

- Roll a pool of d12s equal to **Attribute + Attribute**.
- Every die showing **10+** gives one Success.
- Every die showing **12** also generates **1 Focus**.
- **Profession** replaces Expertise.
- **Human or Ancestry** provides an automatic Base Ability. Ancestries also provide a raised Attribute cap and access to further Ancestry Abilities.
- **Focus** activates Abilities and magic.
- Spells are Magical Abilities. They use no separate mana, spell-slot or power system.

## Attributes

Realms has eight **Attributes**. They describe what a character can accomplish without help from Profession, equipment or particular Abilities.

- **Agility** — balance, speed, riding, stealth and manual dexterity.
- **Awareness** — vigilance, tracking, judging distance and detecting the hidden.
- **Combat** — weapon training, melee, shooting and tactics under pressure.
- **Cunning** — lies, disguises, thievery, ambushes and improvisation.
- **Lore** — history, medicine, alchemy, crafts, languages and occult knowledge.
- **Might** — strength, endurance, lifting, running and resisting physical strain.
- **Presence** — empathy, persuasion, leadership, charm and reading a room.
- **Will** — courage, self-control, resistance to terror and command of magic.

### Choose Attributes

Every Attribute begins at 1. Distribute 10 additional points among them, for a total of **18 Attribute points**. Each increase of one step costs one point.

A starting character cannot have more than 4 in an Attribute. A value of 1 is a genuine weakness.

The normal **Attribute cap** is 4. Each Profession and each nonhuman Ancestry raises one fixed Attribute cap to 5. This does not provide a free increase. It allows the character to exceed ordinary mortal limits through later advancement.

Ancestry and Profession cap increases do not stack. If both affect the same Attribute, its cap remains 5. Values above 5 are reserved for supernatural beings.

### Common combinations

The GM chooses the two Attributes that best fit the action. The same problem may allow different combinations depending on the character's approach.

| Action                          | Attributes           |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Force open a gate               | _Might + Might_      |
| Fight with a heavy melee weapon | _Combat + Might_     |
| Fight with a quick melee weapon | _Agility + Combat_   |
| Shoot a bow or gun              | _Awareness + Combat_ |
| Sneak past a guard              | _Agility + Cunning_  |
| Track in the wilderness         | _Awareness + Lore_   |
| Deceive or disguise yourself    | _Cunning + Presence_ |
| Persuade or inspire             | _Presence + Will_    |
| Examine alchemy or old magic    | _Awareness + Lore_   |
| Cast a learned formula          | _Lore + Will_        |
| Resist terror                   | _Will + Will_        |
| Treat a severe wound            | _Agility + Lore_     |

Profession determines whether the action becomes easier, not which Attributes are used. Two Huntsmen can therefore track differently: one through learned knowledge with _Awareness + Lore_, another through patience and intuition with _Awareness + Will_.

### Secondary Attributes

- **Resilience** = Might + Will.
- **Stability** = 3.
- **Protection** comes from armour, shields and other defences.

Resilience determines how many points of Damage each row of the wound track contains. Stability determines how much Stress a character can endure before risking a Breakdown.

### Wounds

The wound track has four rows. Each row contains a number of usable boxes equal to the character's Resilience:

1. **Fit:** No penalty.
2. **Wounded:** All actions are one step harder.
3. **Incapacitated:** You fall unconscious and cannot act.
4. **Dying:** You begin making survival tests.

Mark Damage from left to right and continue onto the next row when the current row is filled. Further Damage after the Dying row is filled causes immediate death. Critical Hits can cause injuries, bleeding or Dying regardless of marked Damage. See [[Weapons and Critical Hits]].

## Focus

**Focus** represents resolve, luck and magical power.

- Begin each scene with **2 Focus**.
- Gain **1 Focus** whenever one of your dice shows 12.
- You can hold no more than **5 Focus**.
- Focus is retained within a scene and resets to 2 when a new scene begins.
- An ordinary Ability costs **1 Focus** to activate.
- Particularly strong Abilities and spells may cost more.

Focus cannot normally purchase extra dice or replace other resources. Each use must be tied to a named Ability or spell. The Human Base Ability, Determination, is the exception that allows Humans to spend Focus directly on Attribute tests.

## Profession

**Profession** describes training, experience and a place in the world. When an action clearly falls within the Profession, the test becomes one step easier. Profession also grants access to Abilities unavailable to other characters.

Profession applies to tradecraft, contacts and specialist knowledge. It does not normally make direct attack, defence or casting tests easier. Those benefits come from Abilities.

Each Profession raises the cap of one fixed Attribute to 5:

| Profession | Attribute cap |
| ---------- | ------------- |
| Alchemist  | Lore 5        |
| Huntsman   | Awareness 5   |
| Mercenary  | Combat 5      |
| Thief      | Cunning 5     |
| Vicar      | Presence 5    |
| Witch      | Will 5        |

A Profession is broad. A Huntsman can track, find food, recognise animals and travel through wilderness. A Mercenary can assess weapons, maintain formation, read a battlefield and care for military equipment. Profession does not replace Attributes; it makes actions within its field easier.

See [[Professions]] for the full descriptions and Profession Abilities.

## Ancestry

During character creation, choose **Human** or an **Ancestry**. Ancestry describes a fantastic inheritance that distinguishes the character from ordinary humanity.

Each nonhuman Ancestry provides:

1. An automatic Base Ability.
2. One fixed Attribute cap raised to 5.
3. Access to additional Ancestry Abilities.

Ancestry provides no fixed bonuses or penalties to Attributes. Two Dwarves or two Elves can therefore be entirely different people. The raised cap matters only if the player later develops that Attribute.

| Choice   | Attribute cap | Base Ability    |
| -------- | ------------- | --------------- |
| Human    | None          | Determination   |
| Elf      | Awareness 5   | Elf-Sight       |
| Dwarf    | Might 5       | Unyielding      |
| Halfling | Cunning 5     | Fortune's Child |

### Human

Human is the **non-ancestry**. It provides no fantastic inheritance, raised Attribute cap or access to further Ancestry Abilities. Profession can still raise one Attribute cap to 5.

#### Determination

After making an Attribute test, spend any amount of Focus to add the same number of d12s to the result. Determination can be used only once per test.

Each added die showing 10+ adds a Success. Each 12 also generates Focus as normal. If an added die shows 1, the GM introduces a Complication. Additional 1s increase its severity.

See [[Ancestries]] for the nonhuman Base Abilities and further Ancestry Abilities.

## Abilities

Abilities belong to one of four groups:

- **General Abilities** can be learned by anyone. See [[General Abilities]].
- **Profession Abilities** require a particular Profession. See [[Professions]].
- **Ancestry Abilities** require a particular Ancestry. Humans have no Ancestry Abilities. See [[Ancestries]].
- **Magical Abilities** are spells or supernatural rites conveyed through a pact. See [[Spells]].

An activated Ability follows the same format:

### Ability Name

_Requirement: Profession, Ancestry, Attribute or previous Ability_

**Cost:** 1 Focus

The Ability explains when it can be activated, what happens and how long the effect lasts.

Passive Abilities should be unusual. The important choice at the table is when the character spends Focus.

## Black powder and combat

Black-powder weapons are an established part of warfare, but they have not replaced older arms. Pikes protect gunners. Cavalry can still decide a battle. Crossbows are quieter and more reliable than arquebuses.

Black-powder weapons follow three principles:

- **Deadly:** A hit inflicts high Damage and may penetrate armour.
- **Slow:** The weapon must be reloaded after every shot. Reloading takes several actions without a relevant Ability.
- **Revealing:** The report carries far. Smoke and flame reveal the shooter's position.

Swords, spears, bows and crossbows remain useful because they are faster, quieter and do not depend on dry powder. See [[Weapons and Critical Hits]] for the weapon and injury rules.

## Magic

All magic is conveyed by [[The Others|the Others]]: ancient beings once worshipped as gods. An individual being is called an **Other**. Some resemble fey sovereigns, living elements, old ones or chaos gods, but these are aspects rather than separate magical Sources.

Pagans worship particular Others through inherited customs and communal covenants. Magicians acquire power through more personal pacts. The participants rarely understand these relationships in the same way as the beings they serve.

### Pacts

When a character learns their first Magical Ability, define the pact that grants it. Establishing the pact does not cost an additional Ability. Record four details:

- **The Other:** Its name or title.
- **Aspects:** Two or three things within its influence.
- **Sign:** The unnatural trace accompanying its magic.
- **Demand:** The service, offering or behaviour it habitually expects.

Later spells must broadly fit the Other's Aspects or be granted through events in play. Entering a second pact is a consequential event, not an ordinary advancement choice.

Witches bargain directly with the Others. Alchemists bind their influence into substances, diagrams and prepared vessels. Vicars invoke them through doctrine and recognised rites, sometimes without understanding the true identity of the power answering them.

### Casting

A spell costs Focus when activated. Make an Attribute test only when the result is uncertain, someone resists or the spell's strength matters. Offensive spells commonly use _Lore + Will_ or _Presence + Will_, depending on their method.

Roll one **Price Die** for every Focus spent:

- Each **1** adds one Complication level.
- Each **10+** adds one Power level.
- Other results have no effect.

Power and Complication do not cancel each other. Price Dice never generate Focus and cannot normally be modified or rerolled. If a casting test fails, the spell has no effect and rolled Power is lost, but any Complication still occurs.

Each spell explains how Power can improve its range, duration, strength or number of targets. Complications reflect the nature of the Other and the pact:

| Level              | Consequence                                                                              |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1 — Omen**       | An obvious Sign, brief affliction, unwanted attention or minor demand.                   |
| **2 — Price**      | Stress, injury, damaged equipment, a binding task or immediate danger.                   |
| **3 — Breach**     | A lasting mark, terrible sacrifice, severe transformation or manifestation of the Other. |
| **4+ — Incursion** | The Other reaches directly into the world and permanently changes the situation.         |

The spell may succeed and cause a Complication at the same time. Realms has no separate corruption score. Stress, conditions, debts and permanent marks show what magic has done.

See [[Spells]] for the complete spell rules and lists.

## Create a character

1. Describe the character in one sentence.
2. Choose Human or an Ancestry. Record its raised Attribute cap and automatic Base Ability. Human receives only Determination.
3. Choose a Profession and record its raised Attribute cap.
4. Distribute 18 points among the eight Attributes. Each must be at least 1 and can be no higher than 4 during character creation.
5. Calculate Resilience and Stability.
6. Choose two Abilities:
   - One Ability from the character's Profession, or an available spell.
   - One General Ability or, for a nonhuman character, one Ability from their Ancestry.
7. Choose equipment appropriate to the Profession.

A character who selects a spell as their Profession Ability defines their pact at the same time.

## Removed from the original game

This version does not need:

- Expertise as a separate term.
- Extra-Normal Powers.
- Trauma as the source of powers.
- Momentum determining the starting amount of Focus.
- Stress replacing missing Focus.
- Separate resources for magic.

Trauma can remain a consequence of violence and supernatural encounters, but it is not the source of a character's Abilities.
