# The Others

The **Others** are ancient, inhuman beings worshipped as gods, invoked as angels and condemned as demons. These names describe allegiance and theology, not different kinds of creature.

The dominant Church recognises that many gods exist. It teaches that only the **Ascendant** deserves worship. All other powers are inferior in authority, morality and strength. This is partly true. The Ascendant is among the mightiest and most active of the Others, but it is neither alone nor the strongest.

Four beings stand above the rest: the Ascendant, the Wild, the Flame and the Deep. These **Great Others** each gather **Lesser Others** into a **Court**. Court allegiance is not immutable. Lesser Others defect, submit, change names or serve more than one master. A Lesser Other accepted into the Ascendant's Court becomes an angel in Church doctrine. The same being becomes a demon when it serves a rival.

The four titles are their most widely understood names. Older traditions use other epithets and disagree about each Great Other's shape, gender and intentions.

Most mortal pacts are made with Lesser Others. A direct pact with one of the Four is rare and seldom leaves the magician recognisably human.

## The Ascendancy

Several centuries ago, the cult of the Ascendant allied itself with conquering kings, city magistrates and ambitious nobles. Its priests offered binding oaths, protected roads and sanctuary behind consecrated walls. Its armies broke the great temples of its rivals.

The Church did not erase every local god. Lesser Others willing to submit were given new titles and places within the sacred order. Their shrines became chapels. Their priests became custodians, hermits or saints. Those that resisted were named demons, their rituals outlawed and their names removed from public record.

The three rival Great Others were pushed away from settled lands but never destroyed. The Church's network of bells, altars, laws and repeated rites anchored the Ascendant throughout the settled lands. Generations grew up knowing that other gods existed but believing that only one stood above them.

That order is now weakening. The Wild returns where old boundaries are broken. The Flame spreads through foundries, gunpowder and printing presses. The dreams of the Deep seep from mines, sea routes and forgotten graves. The Ascendant remains powerful, but its monopoly is ending. See [[World and History]] for the Ascendancy and its aftermath.

## The Ascendant

The Ascendant is also called the Crown Above, the Lawgiver and the Conqueror God. Church doctrine claims that the title means the one who stands above all others. Forbidden histories give it another meaning: the one who rose above them.

The Ascendant embodies order, hierarchy, law, sanctuary and legitimate rule. Its greatest gift was the binding oath: a promise witnessed by it could end a feud, secure a road or hold a kingdom together. Its priests built a civilisation in which agreements could outlive the people who made them.

The Ascendant is active, attentive and capable of pursuing plans across human generations. It values peace, but understands peace as obedience to a proper order. It protects those inside the walls and judges those left outside them. Its cruelty appears as certainty rather than malice.

Its Court is the most formal of the four. Every servant has a title, station and prescribed duty. Church theologians present this hierarchy as the natural shape of the universe.

### The White Judge

The White Judge witnesses testimony, verdicts and formal confession. Magistrates and inquisitors invoke it before difficult judgements. Its presence bleaches ink and leaves a taste of chalk on the tongue.

It grants the power to reveal perjury, bind testimony and mark the guilty. It demands that judgement be pronounced even when silence would protect family or friends.

### The Keeper of the Threshold

The Keeper guards doors, city walls, guest-right and places of sanctuary. Old household gods absorbed during the Ascendancy now wear its name. Locks click shut when it draws near, and candle flames lean away from open doors.

It grants wards, safe shelter and power over entry. It demands that sanctuary be honoured once offered and that declared enemies never be welcomed secretly.

### The Standard-Bearer

The Standard-Bearer governs discipline, formations, lawful war and the accounting of the dead. Soldiers hear distant marching when it accepts an offering. Its banners are never depicted without a list of names sewn into the cloth.

It grants courage, command and protection to those who hold their ground. It demands obedience to recognised authority and a proper record of every life spent in its service.

## The Wild

The **Wild** embodies life beyond command: beasts, fertility, hunger, seasons, predation, desire and uncultivated land. It can feed a village, heal exhausted soil or guide a lost child home. It also recognises the rights of wolves, winter and plague.

It is also called the Many-Bodied, the Horned Mother and the Green Hunger. Its manifestations may be male, female, both or neither. They may appear as a single creature or as an entire landscape acting with one will.

The Wild values appetite, territory, kinship and reciprocal obligation rather than human mercy. Its servants enforce old rules of hospitality and blood-price, but acknowledge no permanent law.

The Church calls it the Beast, the Unruled or the Enemy Beyond the Wall. Its Court is not a hierarchy. It behaves like a shifting hunt or ecosystem. Lesser Others follow, challenge and sometimes devour one another.

### The Antlered Mother

The Antlered Mother governs birth, prey animals, milk, blood and the recovery of living things after hardship. She is worshipped by midwives, herders and villages at the edge of famine. Her presence causes old scars to redden and animals to begin giving milk.

She grants fertility, bodily transformation and protection for the young. She demands the first portion of a birth, harvest or kill. What constitutes a portion is not always clear.

### The Summer Wolf

The Summer Wolf governs the hunt, pack loyalty, vengeance and contested territory. Hunters leave it the heart of the first animal taken each season. Its tracks appear warm even in snow.

It grants endurance, pursuit and strength when defending kin. It demands that no useful kill be wasted and that no member of the pack be abandoned without retribution.

### The Briar Bride

The Briar Bride governs desire, hospitality, secret paths and the dangerous boundary between invitation and trespass. She appears in wedding songs older than the villages singing them. Her presence tangles thread and fills locked rooms with the smell of wet roses.

She grants glamour, concealment and passage along roads that do not remain on maps. She demands that invitations be honoured precisely and that gifts never be accepted without repayment.

## The Flame

The **Flame** embodies transformation through intention: fire, lightning, craft, invention, war, passion and revolt. It taught mortals to harden metal and rewards anyone willing to destroy an old form to create a new one.

It is also called the Red Hand, the First Maker and the Rebel Light. Smiths depict it as a craftsperson. Revolutionaries describe it as a prisoner breaking its chains. Both accounts may be true.

The Flame offers useful power. It inspires artificers, alchemists, gunners and political radicals. Its horror is its inability to value preservation. Every institution is fuel, every body unfinished material and every peace an interruption.

The Church calls it the Ashen Enemy. Its resurgence is tied to the new age. Foundries burn through the night. Cannon break ancestral walls. Printing presses multiply forbidden ideas. Most people feeding the Flame have never spoken its name.

### The Smith Without Hands

The Smith Without Hands governs metalwork, mechanisms and inventions that exceed their maker's understanding. Tools grow warm in its presence and unfinished devices move when nobody is watching.

It grants skill, inspiration and command over worked materials. It demands that every great creation replace or destroy something that came before it.

### The Red Scribe

The Red Scribe governs printing, revelation, persuasive words and ideas that spread beyond anyone's control. Printers find unfamiliar sentences set among their type. Readers dream the same words before seeing them on the page.

It grants eloquence, inspiration and the power to reproduce hidden knowledge. It demands circulation. A truth given by the Red Scribe cannot remain private for long.

### Thunder in the Barrel

Thunder in the Barrel governs gunpowder, siege, sudden reversal and death delivered from beyond arm's reach. Gunners honour it with the first measure of powder from a new cask. Its presence leaves black residue beneath clean fingernails.

It grants accuracy, destructive force and courage during bombardment. It demands the first shot in a conflict and punishes weapons kept loaded but unused.

## The Deep

The **Deep** is the strongest of the Four and the least active. It encompasses death, dreams, memory, oceans, caves, silence and the pressure beneath the earth. Everything buried, drowned or deliberately forgotten eventually descends towards it.

It is also called the Unmoving One, the Sleeper Below and the Last Silence. These may be titles given by mortals to the same being, or names for conditions produced by its sleep.

The Deep does not pursue recognisable plans. Its dreams alter tides, awaken the dead and place the same image in a thousand sleeping minds. A deliberate act of the Deep would be difficult to distinguish from a natural law changing.

Its Court is not truly a court. Lesser Others gather around its sleep, interpret its movements and claim to speak on its behalf. Some may be fragments of the Deep. Others are parasites drawing power from it. Their commandments contradict one another because the Deep gives no clear orders.

The Church claims that its founder defeated the Deep during the Ascendancy. The truth may be that the Deep scarcely noticed. The Church sealed entrances, destroyed cults and suppressed names. It did not harm the being beneath them.

### The Drowned Archivist

The Drowned Archivist preserves lost records, drowned cities and memories that no living person can bear. Its libraries appear in dreams as flooded rooms whose shelves descend beyond sight. Water beads on closed books when it is near.

It grants knowledge of forgotten events and words erased from history. It demands memories in payment. The petitioner knows something has been taken but may no longer know what it was.

### The Sleeper in the Last Well

The Sleeper dwells beneath wells, springs and underground rivers. It sends shared dreams during droughts and speaks in the voices of people who drowned elsewhere. Its water reflects stars even beneath a roof.

It grants prophecy, hidden passage and power over sleep or thirst. It demands periods of silence and offerings lowered beyond the reach of any rope.

### The Pale Ferryman

The Pale Ferryman governs passage between life, death and whatever waits below memory. It is invoked at nameless graves and river crossings. An empty boat or an extra set of wet footprints marks its arrival.

It grants speech with the dead, safe passage through haunted places and the postponement of death. It demands proper burial, exact payment and the fulfilment of promises made by the dying.

## Independent Lesser Others

Some Lesser Others refuse every Court. The Four attempt to recruit, bind or redefine them, while the Church often calls independence itself a form of demonic rebellion.

### The Coin-Eater

The Coin-Eater governs bargains, debt, chance and value agreed upon by strangers. Merchants leave bent coins beneath milestones before risky journeys. It grants profitable coincidence and exposes false measures, but demands repayment with interest.

### The Guest at the Hearth

The Guest appears as a stranger arriving shortly before bad weather or violence. It governs hospitality, domestic secrets and the fragile peace beneath a shared roof. It protects hosts and guests who obey the old courtesies. It punishes anyone who asks its true name.

### The Faceless Road

The Faceless Road governs exile, crossroads, missing travellers and destinations reached by the wrong route. It has no shrine beyond cairns whose stones come from distant countries. It grants escape and impossible shortcuts, but those it guides rarely return by the same road.

## Courts and pacts

Court allegiance shapes a pact's Signs, Demands and Complications, but it is not a separate school of magic. Every spell is conveyed by a specific Other.

- A Witch may bargain with an angel, demon, local god or independent power.
- An Alchemist may bind a servant of the Flame while insisting that the process is natural philosophy.
- A Vicar usually invokes the Ascendant or one of its angels, but rival beings sometimes answer accepted prayers under borrowed names.
- A pagan community may maintain a collective covenant without any individual possessing Magical Abilities.

The title applied to an Other reveals the speaker's allegiance more reliably than it reveals the being's nature.
